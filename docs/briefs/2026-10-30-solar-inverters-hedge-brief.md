# FX Hedging Executive Brief

## Exposure

The company is a U.S. solar-inverter maker that expects to receive €5.4 million from an Italian solar developer in 273 days, or about nine months. Since the payment is in euros, the amount the company eventually receives in U.S. dollars will depend on the EUR/USD exchange rate when the payment is received. The main risk is that the euro weakens during those nine months. If that happens, the €5.4 million will convert into fewer U.S. dollars than expected, which could create a meaningful difference in the company’s cash flow.

## Hedge Alternatives

### Forward/Futures

A forward or futures hedge would allow the company to lock in an exchange rate for the €5.4 million receivable before it is received. This would give the company certainty about how many U.S. dollars it will receive and protect against the euro weakening. The downside is that if the euro strengthens instead, the company would not benefit from that favorable exchange-rate movement.

### Money-Market Hedge

A money-market hedge would involve borrowing euros now, converting them into U.S. dollars, and using the future €5.4 million payment to cover the euro obligation. This would help protect the company from changes in the exchange rate and make the dollar value of the receivable more predictable. However, it requires more steps than a forward and would use some of the company’s borrowing capacity.

### Put Option

A put option would give the company the right to sell the euros at a set exchange rate, which would protect the company if the euro weakens. Unlike the other two choices, the company could still benefit if the euro strengthens before the payment is received. The tradeoff is that the company would have to pay an upfront premium for that flexibility, even if the option ends up not being used.

## Assumptions

For this brief, I am assuming the company will receive the full €5.4 million in 273 days and will convert the payment into U.S. dollars. I am also assuming the company’s main goal is to reduce uncertainty around how much the receivable will be worth in dollars. The current spot rate and U.S. and European interest rates still need to be found, so those will be added and compared in the later stages before making a final recommendation.
